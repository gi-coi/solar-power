# Public tenders for solar power in the EU — issues and ongoing debates

Research summary compiled 2026-11-25 from three parallel web-research sub-agents
(policy/rules, China/trade-defence, tender-data/public-body adoption). Full

## 1. What drives public-body demand for solar

- The **EU Solar Energy Strategy** (COM(2022) 221) set >320 GW of PV by 2025 and
  ~600 GW by 2030 (now cited as ≥700 GW). Its **European Solar Rooftops
  Initiative** proposed mandatory rooftop solar on new public/commercial
  buildings.
- Those obligations were legislated in the recast **Energy Performance of
  Buildings Directive (EPBD, 2024)**, Art. 10 — the "EU Solar Standard" — phasing
  in rooftop solar on existing public buildings (schools, hospitals, offices)
  by size from 2028 to 2031.
- **REPowerEU / the Recovery & Resilience Facility** are the main public money:
  RRF is "at the heart" of REPowerEU (~€300bn mobilised), with ~€26bn of
  additional solar-PV investment (2022–2027) estimated. RRF spending is subject
  to a "do no significant harm" test and to state-aid + procurement rules.

This is the *demand* story behind our TED dataset: public bodies are being
pushed — and partly funded — to buy and install solar through tenders.

## 2. The Net-Zero Industry Act (Reg. 2024/1735) — new rules on tenders

The **NZIA** (in force June 2024) is the single biggest change to how public
bodies must tender solar. **Article 25** imposes three layers on public
procurement:

1. **Mandatory environmental-sustainability minimums** (Art. 25(1)) — set by
   implementing act, applicable from **30 June 2026** (reported as Implementing
   Reg. (EU) 2026/718; in practice PV carbon-footprint minimums were still not
   set — a gap industry flags).
2. **At least one additional non-price requirement** (Art. 25(3)) — a social/
   employment condition, **cybersecurity compliance**, or a **timely-delivery**
   obligation.
3. **A resilience / diversification contribution** (Art. 25(7)) — from
   **30 Dec 2025**, triggered where >50% of EU supply comes from one third
   country (for PV cells the threshold is >85%, i.e. China), operationalised by
   an implementing regulation and a Commission Communication on Union supply
   shares (23 May 2025).

**Article 26** covers renewable-energy *auctions* (distinct from classic
procurement): mandatory pre-qualification + sustainability/resilience criteria
on ≥30% of auctioned volume (or 6 GW/year), with a 15–30% weighting (Reg. (EU)
2025/1176). Non-binding guidance followed (22 July 2026).

## 3. The European Solar Charter (April 2024)

A **voluntary** instrument signed by 23 member states + ~100 companies (15 Apr
2024). It commits signatories to early application of NZIA non-price criteria
in auctions *and* public procurement, demand aggregation, and a "Community of
Public Buyers for Sustainable Solar PV". It contains **no trade-defence
measures** (a deliberate compromise — no consensus existed).

## 4. The central debates

### Debate A — Non-price criteria vs. "cheapest panel"
The NZIA's stated goal is to shift procurement "away from lowest price" toward
sustainability/resilience. The contest is over **how hard to push**:

- **SolarPower Europe**: apply resilience as an *award* (not pre-qualification)
  criterion, keep the 15–30% band, prioritise carbon footprint.
- **BDEW / Green Power Denmark**: criteria are "very complex", bureaucratic,
  lengthen timelines, and risk 27 divergent national implementations.
- By Oct 2026 only **six member states** were applying NZIA solar rules (Italy
  furthest — 1.1 GW excluding Chinese cells/modules/inverters). Divergent national
  routes are themselves the debate.

### Debate B — "Made in Europe" / Buy-European vs. single market & WTO-GPA
The sharpest tension. The NZIA resilience criterion and the proposed
**Industrial Accelerator Act (IAA, 2026)** introduce origin/"Made in EU"
preferences that analysts (CEPS) say were built **"inadvertently"**,
instrument-by-instrument, and sit "directly in tension with GPA Article IV(1)"
(the ban on local-content conditions). CEPS: the EU now has a de facto "origin
preference regime". The IAA tries to square this by treating GPA/FTA-partner
content as EU-equivalent. Politically it pits industrial member states against
"end-consumer" member states facing higher costs.

### Debate C — Cheap imports vs. strategic autonomy (China)
- China holds **>80% of every PV manufacturing stage** (95% of wafers; >95% of
  EU panel imports by value, per ESMC); Europe ~1% of global PV manufacturing.
- The 2013–2018 anti-dumping/anti-subsidy duties on Chinese modules lapsed in
  Sept 2018; **no new module/cell trade-defence investigation** has been
  launched (2023–2026). The only live dossier is **solar glass** — duties
  repealed in Oct 2026 because "no Union industry exists".
- The **Foreign Subsidies Regulation** (FSR, Reg. 2022/2560) is the new tool:
  mandatory notification in tenders ≥€250m. Its first solar cases (April 2024)
  targeted Chinese bidders (LONGi/ENEVO, Shanghai Electric) in Romania's
  Rovinari park; **both withdrew** and probes closed without remedies — an
  outcome read as *deterrent* rather than *remedial*.
- **Positions**: manufacturers (ESMC, Meyer Burger) want trade defence +
  resilience preferences; **SolarPower Europe + 420 orgs (#MakeSolarEU) oppose
  trade-defence measures** outright; the Commission signals no new restrictions;
  NGOs focus on forced labour (Xinjiang polysilicon) via the Forced Labour
  Regulation (from Dec 2027).
- **Cost gap is the core**: ESMC itself puts Chinese modules at ~€0.109/Wp vs.
  a ~€0.159/Wp "sustainable European" price (~31% gap).

### Debate D — Cybersecurity of solar components
Inverters are the recognised control-point vulnerability. The NZIA implementing
act "only covers a small fraction of PV installations" and only requires bidders
to "prepare a cybersecurity plan" — it does not let buyers exclude high-risk
suppliers (per the Smart Energy Expert Group). Huawei/Sungrow ≈55% of the
global inverter market; nine of the top ten vendors are Chinese.

### Debate E — Cost & pace of rollout
Buy-European/resilience rules risk raising prices for public bodies and slowing
electrification (which is CAPEX-intensive). The Jacques Delors Centre argues it
"need not break the bank" and the NZIA's ~15% cost escape clause should be
retained. Contested on both sides.

## 5. How public bodies actually tender solar (and the issues)

- **Routes**: framework agreements, **dynamic purchasing systems (DPS)**,
  central purchasing bodies (e.g. LASER/Kent, Portsmouth), and emerging
  **joint/cross-border procurement** (Croatia's ACROSS procured 36 PV plants on
  21 public buildings in one procedure — reported price + staff-time savings).
- **Collective-buying schemes** ("Solar Together", iChoosr) target *households
  and SMEs*, **not** public bodies' own assets — a gap.
- **Barriers**: the ECA (2023) found the 2014 reform had "no demonstrable
  effect" on administrative burden or SME participation; **strategic (green/
  social) criteria are rarely used**; lowest-price award still dominates (>80%
  of awards in eight member states in 2021).

## 6. Data & transparency issues (directly relevant to our dataset)

- **TED data quality**: ECA found ~30% of contracts lack values, ~63% lack
  estimated award value, ~86% lack national registration numbers, and **no
  common supplier identifier** — blocking cross-border/ownership analysis.
- **Below-threshold gap**: only above-threshold notices must be published on
  TED; municipal small contracts live on incompatible national portals. This is
  the stated motivation for the **Public Procurement Data Space (PPDS)** (Sept
  2024) and the **eForms** migration.
- **Critique**: academics (Telles) and think-tanks (Bruegel) argue coverage is
  "vastly different" across sources, green-procurement data is thin, and
  optional eForms fields may not be populated.

## 7. The evidence gap our dataset could fill

No authoritative, **solar-specific** quantification of EU public-body
procurement exists (no reliable count / total value / country breakdown for CPV
0933xxx). Our TED export (~5,000 award×contractor rows) is a candidate
benchmark — **provided** we carry the same caveats the ECA raised: incomplete
values, no common supplier ID, and structural below-threshold under-coverage.

## 8. Takeaway for the story

The cleanest defensible "small story" angle is the collision between two forces
visible in our own data and in policy: **public bodies are being pushed to buy
solar (EPBD standard, EU funds, open tenders) at exactly the moment the rules
for *how* they buy are being rewritten (NZIA non-price/resilience criteria,
Buy-European debates, FSR, cybersecurity)** — and the data to see who is buying
what, where, is itself patchy. Candidate narrower stories: (a) the 60.9%
EU-funded share in our data vs. RRF compliance doubts (ECA 2025); (b) Poland's
~40% share of awards as the largest below-threshold-visible market; (c) the
resilience/China debate localised to a single member state.
